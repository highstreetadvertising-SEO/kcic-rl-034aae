Frequently Asked Questions About Chiropractors in Kennewick (2026)

1. What does a chiropractor treat?

In 2026, chiropractors treat a wide range of conditions including back pain, neck pain, headaches, sciatica, herniated discs, whiplash from auto accidents, sports injuries, carpal tunnel syndrome, and posture-related disorders. Many patients also visit chiropractors for general wellness and preventive care. Chiropractors use spinal manipulation, soft tissue therapy, and rehabilitative exercises to restore proper function to the musculoskeletal and nervous systems.

2. Is chiropractic care safe?

Yes. Chiropractic care is widely recognized as one of the safest non-invasive healthcare disciplines available in 2026. Licensed chiropractors undergo extensive training — typically four years of doctoral-level education — before practicing. Serious adverse events from chiropractic adjustments are extremely rare. Most patients experience mild, temporary soreness after their first few adjustments, which is a normal part of the healing process.

3. How many chiropractic sessions will I need?

The number of sessions varies depending on the nature and severity of your condition. In 2026, many acute conditions respond within 6-12 visits, while chronic or complex conditions may require longer treatment plans. Your chiropractor will conduct a thorough evaluation and provide a personalized treatment plan with a projected timeline during your initial consultation.

4. Does insurance cover chiropractic care in Washington State?

Many health insurance plans in Washington State cover chiropractic care in 2026, including plans from major providers, Medicare, and Medicaid. It's best to contact your specific insurer and your chosen clinic — like Kennewick Chiropractic Injury Clinic — to verify your specific coverage details.

5. What should I expect at my first chiropractic appointment?

Your first chiropractic appointment in 2026 will typically begin with a thorough health history intake and physical examination. 7. What is the difference between a chiropractor and a physical therapist?

Chiropractors focus primarily on diagnosing and treating disorders of the musculoskeletal system, particularly through spinal manipulation and adjustment. Physical therapists focus more on restoring movement and function through therapeutic exercise and movement re-education. In 2026, both disciplines are highly complementary, and some clinics — like Tri-Cities Integrative Health & Chiropractic — offer both under one roof.

8. Is chiropractic care effective for headaches?

Yes. Research in 2026 continues to support chiropractic care as an effective treatment for tension-type headaches and cervicogenic headaches (headaches originating from the neck). Spinal manipulation, particularly of the cervical and upper thoracic spine, has been shown to reduce headache frequency and intensity for many patients. Your chiropractor can determine whether your headaches have a spinal component that may respond to chiropractic treatment.

11. What is spinal decompression therapy?

Spinal decompression therapy is a non-surgical, traction-based treatment that gently stretches the spine to relieve pressure on compressed discs and nerves. In 2026, it is commonly used for conditions including herniated discs, degenerative disc disease, sciatica, and spinal stenosis. The therapy uses a motorized traction table and is typically performed over a series of sessions.
